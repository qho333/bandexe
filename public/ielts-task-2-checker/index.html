How to check your Task 2 essay

  1. Open the writing screen and select Task 2. Enter the whole question, including the instruction about what you must discuss.
  2. Write or paste your essay. Keep your paragraph breaks so you can connect comments to the structure of the answer.
  3. Run the check, then read Task Response first. Ask whether the feedback identifies a missing part of the question or an idea that needs explaining.
  4. Review the paragraph comments and quoted corrections. Rewrite a weak passage yourself before studying the suggested rewrite.

IELTS.org describes Task 2 as at least 250 words in about 40 minutes. Its Academic Writing format guide explains the task and timing. During practice, include planning and checking in your available time instead of spending the entire session producing a first draft.

Read the instruction before choosing your structure

For an agree-or-disagree prompt, write down your position in ordinary language before you draft the introduction. For a discussion prompt, identify the views you must explain and check whether an opinion is also requested. For a two-part question, make a short note answering each part. A familiar essay template can leave a required part unanswered.

Give each body paragraph a specific purpose. State a relevant idea, explain the connection to the question, and use an example that shows how the idea works. The example should support the reasoning. Avoid inventing survey percentages or research findings to make an argument look persuasive.

After drafting, read only the opening sentence of each paragraph. Can you follow the argument? Then read the conclusion beside the introduction. If your position has shifted, decide which version you mean and revise the supporting paragraphs to match.

Worked example: repair, then develop

Illustrative draft: Government should spends more money for buses because traffic is a big problem. A correction is The government should spend more money on buses because traffic congestion is a serious problem. After should, use spend. The phrase spend money on also needs the right preposition.

The sentence still needs a link between spending and congestion. You might continue: More frequent services could make buses a practical alternative for commuters who currently drive. This explains a mechanism. In your essay, check that the surrounding question and argument justify it. One improved sentence cannot determine a whole essay’s band.

Turn feedback into deliberate practice

Group your corrections by cause. If several involve subject–verb agreement, write new sentences with different subjects and check the verbs. If your ideas repeat, label what each paragraph adds and remove material that performs the same job twice. These exercises make the next draft more useful than repeatedly checking an unchanged answer.

For vocabulary, prefer a word that states your meaning precisely over an unfamiliar synonym. For grammar, add a clause when it expresses a reason, condition or contrast you actually need.
