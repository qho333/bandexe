What your writing report gives you

BAND.EXE is a free IELTS writing band score checker that works in your browser. There is no sign-up, payment or advertising. You can make 10 free checks per person per day, and essays are not stored by the site. Results take about 20–60 seconds. Every result is an AI practice estimate, not an official IELTS score.

The report separates Task Achievement or Task Response, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy. You get an estimated band for each criterion and an overall estimate. This helps you distinguish a weak argument from a language problem: adding sophisticated vocabulary will not repair an unanswered question.

Grammar corrections and word-choice upgrades quote your exact words so you can compare the suggestion with your sentence. Per-paragraph comments and a rewrite give you material to study after the score. The exam timer supports timed practice, and export lets you take the report into your revision routine.

Choose the task you are practising

  • Task 2 essays: use the IELTS Task 2 checker to review how your position, explanations and examples answer the question.
  • Academic Task 1: use the IELTS Task 1 checker for reports. Upload the chart image or type a description of its labels, units, figures and main features.
  • General Training Task 1: the same Task 1 guide covers the IELTS letter checker. Include the situation and bullet points so you can assess whether your letter fulfils its purpose.

How to make a check useful

  1. Open the writing screen, select your task type and enter the complete question. For an Academic report, supply the visual information as well as your answer.
  2. Write or paste your draft. If you are practising under time pressure, use the exam timer and leave the original errors in place for review.
  3. Run the check and read the criterion comments before the rewrite. Pick a problem you can point to in your own text.
  4. Revise that passage yourself, then compare it with the suggested rewrite. Export the feedback if you want a record of what you practised.

Read the IELTS Writing marking criteria explained guide if the criterion names feel abstract. Use each comment as a question to investigate, rather than treating a band estimate as a verdict on your ability.

A small correction, with a reason

This teaching example is illustrative: Many student prefers online lessons because it save time. A correction is Many students prefer online lessons because they save time. The plural noun needs students, its verb is prefer, and they clearly refers to the lessons.

Next, develop the idea: Students who commute can use the saved travel time to review their notes. That sentence adds an explanation. It does a different job from fixing agreement, so practise both skills instead of simply copying a polished paragraph.

Put the estimate in context

Scores are calibrated against IELTS.org examiner-marked sample scripts. After calibration, the main checker was within ±1 band of the examiner on 88–90% of small official sample sets of 17–20 scripts. Those small sets do not establish how it will score every new answer or predict an exam result.

Use the feedback to choose your next exercise. For example, if a paragraph lacks support, add a concrete explanation before replacing individual words. For the assessment framework behind the criterion names, see IELTS.org’s scoring explanation.
