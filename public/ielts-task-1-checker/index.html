Academic Task 1: supply the evidence

Open the writing screen and choose Academic Task 1. Add the question, the chart image or a typed description, and your report. If you type the visual information, include the title, labels, units, relevant dates and figures. Describe the chart separately from your answer; using only your report as the source leaves no independent basis for checking its claims.

Before writing, identify the broad picture. Which categories are largest or smallest? Which figures rise, fall or stay similar? Group details that help explain those patterns. A report that lists every value in order can hide the main comparison. Avoid adding causes the visual does not show.

In its Academic Writing format guide, IELTS.org sets out at least 150 words in about 20 minutes for Task 1. Practise reserving some of that time to compare your answer with the visual, especially its units, dates and direction of change.

General Training: use the IELTS letter checker

Choose General Training Task 1 and enter the complete situation and bullet points, then write or paste your letter. First decide who you are writing to and why. A request to a manager and a message to a friend call for different language even when both describe the same event.

Give each requested point enough detail to be useful to the reader. If the prompt asks you to explain a problem, describe its effect and request action, check all those jobs separately. Mentioning a broken heater does not automatically explain its impact or say what you want the recipient to do.

Keep the tone consistent from greeting to closing. Read requests aloud to check whether they sound appropriate for the relationship. The IELTS.org General Training format guide explains the letter task, including its minimum of 150 words and approximate 20-minute allocation.

Use the report in a sensible order

You receive Task Achievement, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy band estimates, plus an overall estimate. Grammar corrections and word-choice upgrades quote your exact words; per-paragraph comments and a rewrite help you study the answer. An exam timer and export support your practice routine.

Start with Task Achievement. For a report, compare any flagged feature with the source chart. For a letter, compare the comments with the requested points. Then examine organisation and sentence corrections. A more fluent rewrite is useful only if it keeps the facts, purpose and meaning you need.

Worked examples for both formats

Illustrative report sentence: The number of visitors were increase. If the chart shows a completed past period, a correction is The number of visitors increased. The verb now describes a past change directly. You still need to verify the direction against the chart and add a relevant comparison or figure from it.

Illustrative letter sentence: I am writing for ask you repair the heater. A correction is I am writing to ask you to repair the heater. For a repair request, follow with the actual problem and a suitable next step. This adds useful information instead of merely making the opening longer.

Frequently asked questions

Can I upload a chart for Academic Task 1?

Yes. Academic Task 1 supports a chart image upload or a typed description. Include the question and your report as well.

What should a typed chart description include?

Include the title, labels, units, dates, relevant figures and main features. Keep the source description separate from the report you want checked.

Can I check a General Training letter?

Yes. Choose General Training Task 1, include the situation and requested points, and add your letter.

Does a Task 1 estimate cover my whole Writing test?

No. It concerns the answer you submitted. It is an AI practice estimate, not an official IELTS score or a result for a complete Writing test.
