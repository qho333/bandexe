How the IELTS Speaking test works

The Speaking test is a conversation with an examiner that lasts 11 to 14 minutes. Part 1 is a short interview about familiar topics such as your studies, your home or your free time. In Part 2 you receive a task card, have one minute to prepare, and then speak alone for up to two minutes. Part 3 is a longer discussion of more general and abstract questions linked to the Part 2 topic. IELTS.org explains the timing in its Speaking test format guide.

Examiners assess four criteria: Fluency and Coherence, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range and Accuracy, and Pronunciation. Each carries equal weight in the speaking band.

What the mock test does

Each test uses a fresh set of questions written in the official format, so you are not memorising the same prompts. The examiner reads every question aloud with a natural voice that runs in your browser. You answer with your microphone, and short answers get a neutral follow-up such as Why do you think that is?, just as a real examiner might ask.

Part 2 gives you the one-minute preparation time with a notes box, then a two-minute timer while you talk. You can take the full test or practise only Part 1, Part 2 or Part 3.

When you finish, the report shows an estimate for each criterion, a quote from your answer with a better version, one concrete tip per criterion, and the area to work on first. Below the report you get the full transcript of what you said, question by question. Your recent results are saved in your browser so you can see whether you are improving.

How close are the estimates?

We tested the marker on 58 sample recordings that had published examiner bands. The overall estimate was within half a band for 28 of them, and about 0.9 of a band away on average. Treat a single result as a rough sign, and look for a pattern over several tests.

Pronunciation is the least certain estimate, because the marker works from a transcript and from words that were hard to recognise. Your accent itself is never marked down. Only lost clarity counts.

Conversation practice with Echo

Echo is a friendly AI partner for everyday speaking. Tap the microphone once and just talk: Echo listens, replies, asks a follow-up question and listens again. When a sentence has a grammar or word-choice mistake, a short You said and Say line shows the corrected version without interrupting the conversation. You can hide the text if you want to practise listening only.

Kids mode offers simpler questions and sentence-building games for younger learners, with a safety check on every message.

Make each practice session count

  1. Answer Part 1 questions in two or three sentences: a direct answer, a reason and a small example.
  2. In Part 2, use your minute to write four or five keywords, not full sentences. Cover every point on the card, then add a short story.
  3. In Part 3, give an opinion, explain why, and compare two sides or two groups of people.
  4. After the test, read your transcript. Mark repeated words and unfinished sentences, then say the same answer again more clearly.

Frequently asked questions

Is the speaking practice free?

Yes. There is no sign-up, payment or advertising. Each connection can have 5 marked mock tests a day, and practice conversations have a separate daily allowance.

Does my accent lower the estimate?

No. The marker is told never to penalise an accent itself, only words that were hard to understand. Pronunciation is the roughest of the four estimates, because it is judged from a transcript.

Can I practise one part at a time?

Yes. Choose the full test, or only Part 1, Part 2 or Part 3 before you start.

What do I need?

A microphone and a recent browser such as Chrome, Edge or Safari. Allow the microphone when the browser asks. You can also type in the conversation practice.

Is there a mode for children?

Yes. Kids mode is for ages 6 to 12. Every message is checked for safety, and children's recordings are not sent for transcription.
